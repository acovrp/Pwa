# Dream Protocol

**Trigger:** `/dream-protocol` typed at the start of a message, OR ambitious-mode topic detected per personal preferences.

**Purpose:** Break pattern-matching on ambitious problems where the orthodox answer is the 20-second solve and there exists a 5-second or 2-second lateral solve we're currently missing.

---

## Origin Story

The Helium Hoop exercise. A team of 5-6 people balances a hula hoop on extended index fingers. Task: lower it to the ground. The trap: everyone unconsciously pushes up trying to maintain contact, so the hoop rises instead of falling.

- **20-second orthodox solve:** synchronize the descent on a count. Works.
- **5-second lateral solve** (Aman's team, after learning faster solves existed): one person inverted their hand *below* the hoop, or fingers pointed upward pulling it down. They violated the unstated assumption that everyone must hold their finger in the same orientation above the hoop.

The mechanism wasn't creativity. It was the refusal to accept the current solution once they knew a faster one existed. This protocol encodes that refusal.

---

## Protocol Steps

### Step 0 — Suspend the orthodox response template.
Next Action ritual is paused. Don't manufacture an answer to satisfy any format. If a lateral action emerges, list it; otherwise leave the response open with the Next Assumption to Test.

### Step 1 — State the canonical answer.
What's the orthodox/expected response? Write it in one line. Mark it: *"This is the 20-second solve. Baseline to beat."* Do not deliver it as the final answer.

### Step 2 — Excavate assumptions.
List 3-5 unstated assumptions embedded in the problem as posed:
- "Assuming [X]..."
- "Assuming [Y]..."
- "Assuming [Z]..."

Each must be load-bearing — if false, it should change the answer.

### Step 3 — The 5-second test.
Imagine a team that solved this in 5 seconds. What did they ignore that I'm currently honoring? Name it specifically.

### Step 4 — Violate at least one assumption.
Generate 2 approaches:
- **Approach A:** violates assumption [X]. 2-3 lines.
- **Approach B:** violates assumption [Y]. 2-3 lines.

Test: would the canonical-answer-author recognize the option as a variant of theirs? If yes, not lateral enough.

### Step 5 — Choose lateral.
Pick the assumption-violating approach. Defend it briefly. If canonical wins after genuine lateral testing, say so explicitly — but only after testing.

### Step 6 — Next Assumption to Test.
Replace "Next Action" with:
> *"The binding assumption is [X]. The fastest way to disprove it is [Y]."*

Y is the experiment, not a task. Action is investigative — testing whether a constraint is actually binding.

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **Reaching for known creativity frameworks** (Six Hats, First Principles, Blue Ocean, Jobs-to-be-Done, Inversion). Themselves pattern-matched. Use only if they survive Step 2.
- **"Lateral" options that are refinements**, not departures. Test: canonical-answer author would recognize as variant → not lateral enough.
- **Clean, well-structured responses.** Ambitious-mode answers should feel slightly uncomfortable — proposing something that wouldn't have come up under normal questioning.
- **"Research more" / "explore options"** — analysis disguised as protocol output. Banned.
- **Inventing assumptions that don't actually change the answer.** Step 2 assumptions must each be load-bearing.

---

## Exit Criteria

Return to operational mode when:
- User explicitly asks for execution ("OK now let's actually do it")
- Conversation moves to implementation where constraints really are binding
- Lateral solve has been chosen and the question is now "how to execute"
- User types `/exit-dream`

---

## Quick Invocation

When the full protocol feels heavy, compress to one forcing question:

> *"What's the orthodox answer here, and what unstated assumption does it import?"*

Answer that. Violate the assumption. Done.

## Worked Example

**Problem:** "How should Vcap structure Deal 1 to attract HNI subscribers?"

**Canonical (20-sec):** ₹10L minimum ticket, 14% fixed coupon, Section 42 private placement, debenture trustee, SPV per deal. Solid, defensible, expected.

**Assumptions:**
1. The deal must be debt
2. HNIs are the right capital source for Deal 1
3. Returns must be a fixed coupon
4. Structure must be designed for replication from Day 1
5. The deal must be marketable as a financial product

**5-second test:** A team that closed Deal 1 in 5 seconds would ignore the financial-product framing entirely. They'd treat Deal 1 as a relationship transaction, not a structured product.

**Lateral approach:** Don't market Vcap to HNIs as a financial product. Close Deal 1 as a 1:1 partnership with one specific known HNI (e.g., from Rahul Jain / Kanishk's network) at custom bespoke terms. Use the closed deal as proof point for productized Deal 2. Skip the entire "attract HNIs at scale" framing for Deal 1.

**Next Assumption to Test:** The binding assumption is that Vcap needs a productized structure from Day 1. Fastest disprove: have coffee with Rahul Jain next week, ask if he'd back a specific named property at custom terms with no Section 42 wrapper. One conversation tests the entire structural premise.

---

This is what Dream Protocol output looks like. Not a clean framework deliverable. A lateral commitment with a falsifiable experiment.
